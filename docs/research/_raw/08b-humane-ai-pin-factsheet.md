## A10. Root-cause evidence
- **(a) No killer use case; tried to replace the phone.** Framed as phone successor (NYT 9 Nov 2023: "...the device that comes after the smartphone"); Ars Nov 2023: "a $700 voice command box [that] definitely won't replace your phone"; shipped without basics (timers, calendar, navigation) and with no phone app; duplicated smartphone functions while charging for a separate data plan. (High)
- **(b) Technology.** ~6 s for simple queries; repeated thermal shutdowns; 9-minute projector cap; 4–5 h battery; projector unreadable in sunlight; hallucinations; old Snapdragon 720G; almost everything in the cloud → latency was structural. (High/Med)
- **(c) Business model.** $699 + $24/mo ($288/yr), tied to a T-Mobile line, US-only; carrier lock blocked resale of returns; ~$9M sales vs ~$230M raised; 100k target missed by ~90%; exit at $116M ≈ half of capital raised and ~1/7 of the price sought. (High)
- **(d) Hype ahead of readiness.** Runway debut, TIME Best Inventions and staged TED demo before shipping; launch video edited after errors; ship date slipped; demo units chilled with ice packs. (High/Med)
- **(e) Leadership.** Criticism discouraged; dissenting engineer fired; CTO stepped back months before launch; single-founder vision. (Med-High)

## Lessons for enterprise buyers (M365 per-seat, US + India, work-only, company-governed)
1. **Bricking is a procurement concern** — 10 days' notice, data deleted, refunds limited to 90 days, support site gone. Expect RFPs to demand: contractual end-of-life notice (6–12+ months), bulk export in open formats, offline/local mode, pro-rata refunds/credits, source/escrow or open-protocol commitments.
2. **Avoid single-carrier cellular in the device** — killed refurbishment/resale (bad for IT-managed fleets) and adds US + India carrier/regulatory complexity → relay through the phone over BLE.
3. **Battery supply chain & recall readiness** — supplier cell defect → recall that outlived the company. Enterprises will want serialised asset tracking, a recall playbook, battery certifications (UN38.3, BIS covered elsewhere).
4. **Don't stack a separate device subscription on the seat** — fold hardware into seat pricing with clear TCO.
5. **No staged demos** — pilot with design partners; publish measured latency and battery figures.
6. HP IQ's "future of work" remit makes HP a possible enterprise competitor or partner. (Med)
